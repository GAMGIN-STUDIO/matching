Memory Game Variant with Minimal Randomness

This variant significantly reduces randomness compared to the classic memory game and introduces:

  • strategy
  • risk management
  • information control
  • tactical decision-making between players

This is not just a modification of the classic memory game, but almost an entirely new memory-based board game.

Game Objective

The game ends when:

  • all pairs are removed from the game
  • or the time limit expires

The winner is the player:

  1. with the fewest skips
  2. if tied, the player with the most points wins

What is a skip

A skip means a forced loss of the player's next turn.

Each player has their own skip counter and also an “actual skip” state for immediate skipping of the next round.

Card States

Each card can be in one of three states:

  1. Face-down
  2. Held (revealed on the table)

    The card remains visible between turns because of the player's decision.

  3. Removed from the game

    The pair has been found and removed.

Basic Player Turn

During their turn, a player:

  • may flip a card
  • may choose to do nothing
  • the turn ends when the turn timer expires

Held Card

What is a hold

A held card is a card that remained revealed on the table from a previous turn.

A hold is created only when:

  • the player flips the first card
  • and decides not to flip a second card

Situations After Flipping the First Card

After flipping the first card, two situations may occur.

Situation A — the matching pair is already held

If the second card of the pair is already revealed on the table:

Mandatory pair removal

The pair must immediately be removed from the game.

A1 — player found another player's held card

If the held card was revealed by another player:

  • +1 skip
  • forced skipping of the next turn

A2 — player found their own held card

If the player is also the owner of the held card:

  • the pair is removed
  • the player gains +1 point
  • the player may immediately continue with another turn

This is the primary way of earning points.

Important hold limitation

A player cannot receive a hold reward during the same turn in which the hold was created.

The card must survive at least one full round as a held card.

Situation B — the matching pair is not revealed

If the matching card is not held, the player has two options.

Option 1 — flip the second card

If a pair is created
  • the pair is removed from the game
If no pair is created
  • both cards are turned face-down again

Option 2 — do not flip the second card

  • the first card remains revealed
  • it becomes a held card

This sacrifices an immediate attempt at making a pair in exchange for strategic information for future turns.

Hold Rules

A hold can only be created immediately

The player must decide right after flipping the first card.

It is not possible to:

  • flip two cards
  • and only then decide which one remains revealed

You cannot maintain multiple holds simultaneously

If a player already has a held card from a previous turn:

  • they cannot create a new hold
  • until the original hold is:
    • hidden again
    • or removed by forming a pair

Behavior while already holding a card

If a player has a hold from a previous turn:

  • they may freely flip only one new card
  • if no pair is formed:
    • the newly flipped card is automatically hidden
    • the original hold remains

However, if the player first hides their original hold:

  • they regain the standard ability to flip two cards during the turn
  • at the cost of losing the original hold

Rule Preventing Full Board Reveal

Players cannot endlessly chain holds without resolving previous ones.

This means:

  • it is not possible to continuously add new revealed information
  • without dealing with older holds

The goal is to:

  • prevent the table from becoming overloaded with revealed cards
  • preserve strategy and memory as the core mechanics

Points

Points serve:

  • as a secondary win condition
  • and as an in-game currency for special actions

Reveal Mode

A player may spend:

  • 1 point to temporarily reveal one card

Rules

  • the card automatically hides again after a short time
  • a reveal does not count as a normal free flip
  • the player may repeatedly use reveal mode as long as they have enough points

Combining reveal mode with a normal turn

  • even while using reveal mode, the player still keeps their normal free flips
  • the standard free limit is two flips per turn

If the player wants to continue using the normal mode, they must disable reveal mode.

Removing a Skip

If a player has at least 3 points:

  • they may spend 3 points
  • to remove one skip
  • including the “actual skip” turn penalty

Special Skip Rules at the Start of the Game

To make the opening fairer:

  • with 2 players, skips are disabled for the first 2 rounds
  • with 3 players, skips are disabled for the first 3 rounds

Pair removal still functions normally.

The only exception is reveal mode.

Ownership of Revealed Cards

A revealed card may only be voluntarily hidden by the player responsible for revealing it.

Game Strategy

The game is not only about finding pairs.

Players frequently consider:

  • whether to take risks
  • whether to create a hold
  • whether to remove a pair
  • whether to avoid a skip
  • or whether to do nothing and wait

Strategic Consequences

Player with a low number of skips

If a player has significantly fewer skips than the others:

  • their greatest enemy becomes time
  • it is often better to play safely
  • and sabotage opponents carefully and indirectly

What Makes This Variant Interesting

1. Memory matters more than in classic memory games

Held cards remain on the table and create long-term information.

2. Real strategy emerges

A player may decide to:

  • earn points
  • leave information for others
  • avoid penalties
  • or wait strategically

3. Randomness is significantly reduced

In classic memory games, many turns are purely random.

Here, most turns represent a conscious strategic decision.
